Lesion

Time scale

The lesion is hidden while a case is running.

Ischemia develops over about 20 s after occlusion and resolves with a time constant of about 8 s after reperfusion. Rate and AV conduction follow the territory; lost contraction and slow rates lower blood pressure, and lower pressure perfuses every coronary bed less.

Management

IV fluid Nitroglycerin Atropine Pacing Balloon pump Impella

Support changes what the patient can tolerate and how much oxygen the heart asks for; it does not open the artery. Each choice also has a cost: pressors add afterload, inotropes add demand and ectopy, volume congests a failing left ventricle, nitrates drop the preload a right ventricle depends on.

Open in cath lab→

Orders take clinical time to reach the artery (about 2 minutes pass per second at 1×), and muscle that stays ischemic dies as it waits. PCI stents the lesion; a fibrinolytic opens an occlusion in about half of patients and leaves a stenosis; a bypass feeds the vessel beyond the lesion and leaves the lesion itself.

Patient

Rhythm

Conduction

What the patient brought with them. Fibrillation takes away the P waves, so the AV node shows only as a slowing of the irregular response. A left bundle branch block pushes every ST segment away from its QRS before any lesion does, so read it for change: concordant ST, or discordant elevation out of proportion to the S wave.

Volume and drugs

Both ramp in over about 10 s. What follows is the patient's own response: watch the rate, the pressure and the pulse pressure.
