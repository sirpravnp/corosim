Drawn from CoroSim's own coronary tree and solved flows (spikes/cathlab/export.ts). Schematic, not a clinical image.

Pressure wire pullback (adenosine), LAD

Angiogram: each vessel is projected from the C-arm angle you pick; iodine thickness adds along the ray, so overlap and foreshortening come out of the geometry. The contrast front travels at each vessel's solved blood velocity, so a tight lesion fills late (TIMI 2) and an occlusion stops it (TIMI 0). Echo: a real CT-derived four-chamber heart (the average shape of a statistical model fitted to healthy adults; Rodero et al. 2021, CC BY 4.0, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.4593739) is cut by a transesophageal imaging plane set by the probe position and the omniplane angle; each wall segment moves according to the ischemia the simulation computed for the artery that supplies it, and the labels are read off the model. With labels on, the angiogram shows every vessel opacified. Neither image comes from acoustic or X-ray physics, and the view orientations should be checked by a clinician before any teaching use. Omniplane angles are measured in this model's own frame, so they will not match a probe's readout exactly; views are placed from anatomic landmarks (the four-chamber plane holds the long axis through the inferoseptal and anterolateral walls, the long-axis plane holds the aortic valve, and so on). The vessel stubs beyond the mesh (great veins, pulmonary veins, appendage, ascending aorta, arch, descending aorta) are drawn as simple tubes and are not anatomically measured.
